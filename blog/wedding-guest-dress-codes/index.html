Wedding Guest Dress Codes Explained: What to Wear to Every Wedding

Black tie, cocktail, semi-formal, beach and casual: what each wedding dress code means, outfit ideas for men and women, and colours to avoid.

A wedding invitation often comes with a dress code that sounds clear until you try to get dressed. What's the difference between "formal" and "black tie optional"? Can you wear jeans to a "casual" wedding? This guide explains every common dress code with practical outfit ideas.

First, read the invitation (and the website)

The dress code is usually in the bottom corner of the invitation or on the wedding website. If it isn't, look for clues:

  • Time of day: evening weddings (after 6 pm) are usually more formal than daytime ones.
  • Venue: a ballroom or cathedral suggests formal; a barn, garden or beach suggests relaxed.
  • Invitation style: engraved card stock and calligraphy hint at formal; a playful design hints at casual.

When in doubt, ask someone in the wedding party. Overdressing slightly is always better than underdressing.

White tie

The most formal dress code, and rare.

  • Men: black tailcoat, white wing-collar shirt, white bow tie and waistcoat, patent shoes.
  • Women: a floor-length evening gown, elegant jewellery, optional long gloves.

Black tie

Formal evening wear.

  • Men: a tuxedo (black or midnight navy), white dress shirt, black bow tie, black patent or highly polished shoes.
  • Women: a floor-length gown, or an especially elegant cocktail dress or dressy jumpsuit in luxurious fabric.

Black tie optional

The couple will be in formal wear and you're welcome to be, but it isn't required.

  • Men: a tuxedo, or a dark, well-tailored suit (navy, charcoal or black) with a tie.
  • Women: a floor-length gown, a formal midi or cocktail dress, or elegant separates.

Formal

Similar to black tie optional; many couples use the terms interchangeably.

  • Men: a dark suit with a tie; a tuxedo is welcome for evening weddings.
  • Women: a long dress, a dressy cocktail dress or a formal jumpsuit.

Cocktail

The most common wedding dress code.

  • Men: a suit in any classic colour (navy, grey, a lighter shade for daytime) with a tie or a polished open collar if the invitation feels relaxed.
  • Women: a knee-length or midi dress, an elegant jumpsuit or dressy separates. Heels, block heels or dressy flats.

Semi-formal

Slightly more relaxed than cocktail.

  • Men: a suit or a blazer with smart trousers; a tie is optional for daytime.
  • Women: a midi or knee-length dress, a skirt and blouse, or a smart jumpsuit.

Beach or destination formal

Smart, but made for heat and sand.

  • Men: a light suit in linen or cotton (tan, light grey, light blue), an open collar or a light tie, loafers or smart sandals. No socks needed with loafers.
  • Women: a breezy maxi or midi dress in a light fabric, with flat or wedge sandals (heels sink into sand).

Casual or "dressy casual"

Casual doesn't mean what you'd wear to the supermarket.

  • Men: chinos or smart trousers with a button-up shirt; a blazer is a good idea. Clean loafers or leather sneakers.
  • Women: a sundress, a skirt with a nice top, or smart trousers with a blouse.

Avoid jeans unless the couple specifically says they're welcome.

Colours to avoid

  • White, ivory, cream and very pale champagne unless the couple asks guests to wear white.
  • The bridal party's colour, if you know it.
  • Cultural considerations: red is a traditional bridal colour in many South Asian and Chinese weddings, and black or white can have other meanings in some cultures. If you're attending a wedding from a tradition you're less familiar with, ask.

For pairing colours confidently, see our colour combinations guide.

Practical tips

  • Check the weather and venue. Outdoor ceremonies need a wrap or jacket, and shoes that work on grass.
  • Comfort matters. You'll likely stand, sit, eat and dance for eight hours.
  • Plan early. Tailoring takes one to two weeks.
  • Pack smart for destination weddings. Our 7-day packing guide shows how to bring a formal outfit without overpacking.
