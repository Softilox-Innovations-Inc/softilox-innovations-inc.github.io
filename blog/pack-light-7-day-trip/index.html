How to Pack Light for a 7-Day Trip (Carry-On Only Packing List)

Pack for a week in a carry-on: the 5-4-3-2-1 method, a complete 7-day packing list, how to choose a colour palette for travel, and folding and rolling tips.

Packing light feels impossible until you've done it once. Then you'll never go back: no checked-bag fees, no waiting at the carousel, and no dragging a suitcase up four flights of stairs. The secret isn't owning tiny clothes. It's packing a mini capsule wardrobe where everything works together.

Step 1: Check the weather and your plans

Look at the forecast for your destination and list what you'll actually do: sightseeing, beach days, a business meeting, a nice dinner, hiking. Every outfit you pack should serve at least one of these, and ideally two. Our temperature guide helps translate a forecast into clothes.

Step 2: Pick a travel colour palette

This is the step that makes carry-on travel work. Choose:

  • One or two base neutrals for bottoms, outerwear and shoes (navy and tan, or black and grey).
  • One light neutral for tops (white or cream).
  • One accent colour for personality.

When every top matches every bottom, five tops and four bottoms make twenty outfits. Our capsule wardrobe guide explains how to choose a palette.

Step 3: Use the 5-4-3-2-1 method

A simple formula for seven days:

  • 5 tops
  • 4 bottoms (including shorts or skirts if it's warm)
  • 3 pairs of shoes (including the pair on your feet)
  • 2 swimsuits or layers
  • 1 hat or key accessory

Adjust for your trip. A cold-weather city break swaps swimsuits for a second knit; a beach week might need two more tops and one fewer pair of trousers.

Shoes

  • One comfortable walking pair (wear these on the plane)
  • One smarter pair that works with your evening outfit
  • Sandals or flip-flops if it's warm

Accessories

  • A scarf (warmth, sun cover, instant outfit change)
  • A belt that works with both trousers
  • Sunglasses and a packable hat
  • A compact tote or day bag

Packing techniques that save space

  • Wear your bulkiest items on the plane: boots, jacket and your heaviest knit.
  • Roll soft clothes like tees, jeans and knits to save space and reduce creases.
  • Fold structured items (shirts, blazers) flat on top, or use a garment folder.
  • Use packing cubes to compress clothes and keep things organised.
  • Fill shoes with socks and small items.
  • Decant toiletries into small containers; most hotels supply the basics.

Re-wear without anyone noticing

You don't need seven different outfits. Bottoms, outer layers and shoes can be worn several times. Change the top and accessory and the outfit reads as new. A quick sink wash with travel detergent halfway through the trip refreshes tees and underwear.

Special cases

Formal event. Pack one smart outfit that can be dressed down for other days, for example a blazer you'll also wear with jeans, or a dress that works with sneakers during the day. If it's a wedding, check our wedding dress code guide.

Cold destinations. Layers beat bulk: thermal base layers, a merino knit and a packable down jacket fit in a carry-on more easily than one thick coat.

Business trips. One suit or blazer, two or three shirts, one pair of smart shoes. The blazer doubles as your evening layer.

Quick summary

  1. Check the forecast and your plans.
  2. Choose a two- or three-colour palette.
  3. Follow 5-4-3-2-1 and adjust for climate.
  4. Wear the bulkiest items on the plane.
  5. Roll soft clothes, fold structured ones.
  6. Re-wear bottoms and layers; change tops and accessories.
