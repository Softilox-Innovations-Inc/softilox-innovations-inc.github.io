Cost Per Wear: How to Calculate It and Shop Smarter

What cost per wear means, the simple formula, real examples comparing cheap and quality clothes, and how to use it to buy less and waste less.

By The Wearly Style Team · Updated November 24, 2026 · 3 min read

  2. Worked examples
  3. How to estimate before you buy
  4. Using cost per wear to shop smarter

The cheapest item in a shop is rarely the cheapest item in your closet. A $25 shirt you wear twice cost you $12.50 each time. A $90 shirt you wear 60 times cost $1.50. That's the idea behind cost per wear: a simple number that measures what clothes actually cost you.

The cost per wear formula

Cost per wear = Price ÷ Number of times worn

That's it. For a more complete picture, add what it costs to look after the item:

Cost per wear = (Price + care and repair costs) ÷ Times worn

Dry-cleaning a suit five times a year adds up, and so does resoling good shoes, though resoling usually extends their life enough to be worth it.

Worked examples

Item Price Times worn Cost per wear
Fast-fashion party top $30 2 $15.00
Quality white T-shirt $35 70 $0.50
Wool overcoat $280 150 (five winters) $1.87
Trend jacket $120 6 $20.00
Leather boots (resoled once) $220 + $60 300 $0.93
Wedding-guest dress $150 1 $150.00

The pattern is clear: versatile basics and well-made outerwear and shoes come out cheapest over time. One-off and trend pieces come out most expensive.

How to estimate before you buy

You can't know exactly how often you'll wear something, but you can estimate honestly:

  1. How often does this kind of item get worn? Jeans: weekly. A sequinned jacket: a few times a year.
  2. How many things in my closet does it go with? If it pairs with fewer than three items, it'll be worn less.
  3. How long will it last? Check the fabric, stitching and reviews.
  4. Does it suit my actual life? A beautiful formal dress is less useful if you go to one formal event a year.

Then divide. If the estimated cost per wear is high, ask whether the item is worth it for the occasion, or whether you could borrow, rent or buy second-hand instead.

Using cost per wear to shop smarter

  • Invest in the things you wear most: shoes, coats, jeans, everyday knits and bags.
  • Spend less on trend pieces and items for one-off events.
  • Favour versatile colours. A navy blazer works with far more than a lime-green one (see our colour combinations guide).
  • Look after what you own. Washing less often, at lower temperatures, and repairing early can double an item's life and halve its cost per wear.

Cost per wear and sustainability

Making clothes uses a lot of water, energy and raw materials. Wearing each item more often is one of the most effective things an individual can do to reduce their fashion footprint. Cost per wear is a sustainability metric dressed up as a money-saving one.

The hidden cost: clothes you never wear

Most closets contain pieces that have never been worn, or worn once. Their cost per wear is the full price. Rather than feeling guilty, use them:

  • Style them differently. Try them with pieces you hadn't considered.
  • Give them a deadline. If an item isn't worn in the next three months, sell or donate it so someone else wears it.
  • Learn from them. What made you buy it? A sale? A trend? A fit you hoped would work? That's a pattern to watch.

Key takeaways

  • Cost per wear = price ÷ times worn.
  • Cheap clothes worn rarely are expensive; good clothes worn often are cheap.
  • Estimate before buying: frequency, versatility, durability and fit with your life.
  • Care and repair lower cost per wear more than you'd think.
  • The most expensive clothes are the ones you never wear.
