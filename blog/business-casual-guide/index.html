Business Casual Explained: What to Wear for Men and Women

What business casual means in 2026: clear do's and don'ts, outfit formulas for men and women, and how to adapt it for any season or office.

"Business casual" might be the most confusing dress code in the modern workplace. It means something slightly different in a bank, a tech start-up and a school, and it has relaxed a lot in the past decade. Here's a clear, practical guide.

What business casual means

Business casual sits between business formal (suits and ties) and casual (T-shirts and jeans). The aim is to look polished and professional without the formality of a suit.

Think: tailored, clean, well-fitted, not sporty.

The core pieces

For men

  • Trousers: chinos, tailored wool or cotton trousers; dark jeans where allowed.
  • Tops: button-down shirts (oxford, poplin, chambray), polo shirts in good fabric, fine-knit sweaters.
  • Layers: an unstructured blazer, a cardigan, a quarter-zip knit.
  • Shoes: loafers, derbies, brogues, chelsea boots, clean leather sneakers.

For women

  • Bottoms: tailored trousers, chinos, knee-length or midi skirts, dark jeans where allowed.
  • Tops: blouses, button-up shirts, fine knits, shell tops under a blazer.
  • Dresses: sheath, wrap or shirt dresses at or below the knee.
  • Layers: blazers, cardigans, tailored jackets.
  • Shoes: loafers, ballet flats, block heels, ankle boots, clean leather sneakers.

Business casual do's and don'ts

Do Don't
Wear well-fitted, pressed clothes Wear wrinkled or stained clothing
Choose neutral bases (navy, grey, camel, white) Wear loud graphics or slogans
Add a blazer for meetings Wear hoodies or athletic wear
Keep jeans dark and clean (if allowed) Wear ripped, faded or distressed denim
Wear leather shoes or minimal sneakers Wear flip-flops or running shoes
Keep accessories simple Wear anything too revealing for your office

Outfit formulas that always work

For men

  1. Navy blazer + white oxford shirt + grey trousers + brown loafers
  2. Light blue shirt + navy knit + tan chinos + suede desert boots
  3. Grey merino sweater + white shirt collar + dark jeans + brown chelsea boots
  4. Chambray shirt + olive chinos + white leather sneakers
  5. Polo in a fine knit + navy trousers + brown loafers

For women

  1. Camel blazer + white tee + black tailored trousers + loafers
  2. Silk blouse + midi skirt + block heels
  3. Fine-knit sweater + wide-leg trousers + ankle boots
  4. Shirt dress + belt + low heels or flats
  5. Striped top + navy blazer + dark jeans + ballet flats

A five-day business casual rotation

You don't need a huge wardrobe. With about 12 to 15 well-chosen pieces you can go weeks without repeating an outfit:

  • 2 pairs of trousers (grey and navy or black)
  • 1 pair of chinos (tan or olive)
  • 1 pair of dark jeans
  • 4 to 5 tops (shirts, blouses, knits)
  • 1 to 2 blazers or cardigans
  • 2 pairs of shoes

Adapting for the season

Hot weather: linen and cotton trousers, short-sleeve button-ups or shell tops, unlined blazers, loafers without socks. Light colours keep you cooler.

Cold weather: wool trousers, merino knits over collared shirts, a smart wool coat, leather boots. Layers matter because offices are often warm inside.

Adapting for your industry

  • Finance, law, client-facing: lean towards the formal end. Blazers most days, minimal jeans.
  • Tech and start-ups: lean casual. Dark jeans, knits and clean sneakers are often fine.
  • Creative industries: more room for colour, pattern and statement pieces, still well-fitted.
  • Education and healthcare admin: practical and comfortable, with a smart layer for meetings.

When starting a new job, dress at the more formal end for the first week, then adjust to what your colleagues wear.
