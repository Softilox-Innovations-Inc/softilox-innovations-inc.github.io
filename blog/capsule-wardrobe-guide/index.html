How to Build a Capsule Wardrobe: A Step-by-Step Guide with Checklist

Build a capsule wardrobe that works: how many pieces you need, choosing a colour palette, a full checklist, and 30+ outfits from 30 items.

A capsule wardrobe is a small collection of clothes that all work together. Instead of a closet full of pieces that only match one other thing, you keep a focused set where almost every top goes with almost every bottom. The result is fewer decisions in the morning, more outfits from less clothing and a lot less "I have nothing to wear".

This guide walks you through building one from the clothes you already own, step by step, with a checklist you can copy.

What is a capsule wardrobe?

The term was popularised in the 1970s by London boutique owner Susie Faux and later made famous by designer Donna Karan's "Seven Easy Pieces" collection. The idea is simple: a limited number of versatile, high-quality essentials that never go out of style, topped up with a few seasonal pieces.

A capsule is not a uniform and it doesn't have to be beige. It's a set of clothes chosen to mix and match. A good rule of thumb is that each piece should create at least three different outfits with things you already own.

How many pieces do you need?

There's no magic number, but most people land between 30 and 40 pieces per season. Here's a balanced starting point for a four-season capsule:

Category Pieces Examples
Tops 10–12 T-shirts, shirts, knitwear, blouses
Bottoms 5–7 Jeans, trousers, chinos, skirts
Dresses or suits 2–3 A day dress, a smart dress or a suit
Layers 3–4 Blazer, denim jacket, cardigan
Outerwear 2 A coat and a light jacket or trench
Shoes 4–5 Sneakers, boots, smart shoes, sandals

Don't count underwear, sleepwear, activewear or accessories. And don't treat the numbers as rules: a parent who works from home needs a very different capsule from a lawyer who commutes on foot.

Step 1: Find out what you actually wear

Before you buy anything, look at your habits. For two weeks, note what you wear every day. Most people discover they rotate around 20% of their closet. Those pieces are the start of your capsule because they already fit your life.

Step 2: Choose a colour palette

A capsule works because the colours work together. Build yours in three layers:

  1. Base neutrals (2–3): the backbone, used for bottoms, outerwear and shoes. Navy, charcoal, black, camel, olive and denim blue are all good choices.
  2. Light neutrals (1–2): white, cream, light grey or pale blue for shirts and tees.
  3. Accents (1–2): colours that flatter you and add personality, such as burgundy, forest green, rust or cobalt.

If you're unsure what goes together, our guide to colour combinations for clothes covers the pairings that work every time.

Step 3: Edit your closet

Take everything out of one category at a time and sort it into four piles:

  • Keep: fits well, in good condition, worn in the last year, works with your palette.
  • Maybe: you like it but it only matches one thing. Box it up for three months; if you don't miss it, let it go.
  • Repair: a missing button or a hem that needs taking up. Fix it this month or move it on.
  • Let go: donate, sell or recycle.

Be honest about fit. A capsule has no room for "when I lose five pounds" clothes.

Step 4: Fill the gaps, slowly

Now compare what's left with the table above. You'll usually find a few gaps: maybe you have six pairs of jeans but no smart trousers, or plenty of tops but no layer that works for the office.

Write a short list and buy one piece at a time, prioritising items that unlock the most new outfits. A navy blazer, for example, instantly dresses up jeans, chinos, a white tee and a knit. Step 5: Test it for a month

Live with your capsule for four weeks. Keep a note of moments when you felt stuck, such as nothing warm enough for a cold snap or nothing to wear to a dinner. Those are your real gaps. Everything you didn't reach for is a candidate to swap out next season.

The capsule wardrobe checklist

Use this as a starting point and adjust to your climate and lifestyle.

Tops

  • 3 plain T-shirts (white, grey, navy or black)
  • 2 button-up shirts (white and light blue)
  • 2 knit sweaters (one fine merino, one chunky)
  • 1 striped top
  • 2–3 tops in your accent colours

Bottoms

  • 1 pair dark wash jeans
  • 1 pair light or black jeans
  • 1 pair tailored trousers
  • 1 pair chinos or a skirt

Layers and outerwear

  • 1 blazer (navy is the most versatile)
  • 1 casual jacket (denim or chore jacket)
  • 1 warm coat
  • 1 trench or rain jacket

Shoes

  • White leather sneakers
  • Leather boots
  • One smart pair (loafers, oxfords or heels)
  • Seasonal shoes (sandals or weatherproof boots)

How to get 30+ outfits from 30 pieces

The maths works in your favour. Ten tops and five bottoms already give you 50 combinations before you add layers or shoes. The trick is making sure the combinations actually look good, which comes down to three things:

  • Contrast: pair light with dark, or a texture with a smooth fabric.
  • Proportion: a relaxed top with slim bottoms, or a fitted top with wider trousers.
  • One focal point: let one piece (a print, a colour or a statement jacket) lead and keep the rest quiet.

Common capsule wardrobe mistakes

  • Buying a "starter capsule" from scratch. Start with what you own. A capsule is an edit, not a shopping trip.
  • Going too minimal. If you're doing laundry every two days, you need a few more basics.
  • Ignoring your real life. If you go to the gym five times a week and an office once, your capsule should reflect that.
  • Forgetting the seasons. Swap 8–10 pieces in and out as the weather changes rather than keeping everything out all year.

Final thoughts

A capsule wardrobe isn't about owning as little as possible. It's about owning things you love that work together, so getting dressed takes seconds. Start by noticing what you already wear, choose a palette, edit honestly and fill gaps slowly.
