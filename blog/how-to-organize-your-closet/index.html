How to Organize Your Closet: A 7-Step Declutter and Organization Guide

Organise your closet in a weekend: a 7-step declutter method, what to keep, how to hang, fold and arrange clothes, and habits that keep it tidy.

A cluttered closet makes every morning harder. You can't see what you own, you forget about good pieces, and you end up buying duplicates. Organising it properly takes a weekend, and the right habits keep it that way.

Step 1: Empty it, one category at a time

Don't pull out everything at once; the pile becomes overwhelming. Start with one category, such as tops, then trousers, then dresses, then outerwear, then shoes.

Seeing every T-shirt you own in one pile is usually eye-opening. Most people have far more than they wear.

Step 2: Sort into four piles

For each item, decide:

  • Keep: it fits, it's in good condition, you've worn it in the last year, and you like it.
  • Maybe: you're unsure. Put these in a box with a date three months from now.
  • Repair or alter: missing buttons, a loose hem, a fit that a tailor could fix. Set a deadline.
  • Let go: donate, sell, or recycle textiles that are too worn to reuse.

Questions that help:

  • Would I buy this again today?
  • Does it go with at least three other things I own?
  • Have I avoided wearing it because of fit, comfort or care?

Step 3: Clean the space

With the closet empty, vacuum, wipe the shelves and rails, and check for moth damage. It's also the moment to replace mismatched hangers.

Step 4: Choose what to hang and what to fold

Hang Fold
Shirts and blouses Knitwear and sweaters
Blazers and jackets T-shirts
Dresses and skirts Jeans
Tailored trousers Activewear
Coats Loungewear

Use matching slim hangers. They save space and make the rail look calm. Use wooden or padded hangers for jackets and coats so they keep their shape.

File-fold drawer items (fold and stand them upright) so you can see everything at once instead of digging through a stack.

Step 5: Arrange for how you get dressed

  • Most-worn items at eye level and closest to the door.
  • Group by category, then by colour from light to dark. You'll see gaps and duplicates instantly.
  • Seasonal items go on high shelves or under the bed in breathable bags.
  • Shoes on a rack or in clear boxes; store boots with shapers.
  • Accessories in shallow trays or on hooks, not tangled in a drawer.

Step 6: Digitise your wardrobe

The best-organised closet still hides things behind doors and in drawers. A digital closet puts everything in one view, on your phone, wherever you are.

With Wearly, you take a photo of a pile of clothes and AI separates each item into its own photo, then tags it by category, colour, pattern and season. Once it's in, you can:

  • See your whole wardrobe at a glance, sorted by colour, wear count or cost per wear.
  • Spot items you never wear (and decide whether to style them or let them go).
  • Check what you own while shopping, so you don't buy a fourth navy sweater.
  • Get daily outfits built from everything, not just what's at the front of the rail.

Step 7: Keep it organised

  • One in, one out: when you buy something, let something go.
  • The hanger trick: turn all hangers backwards; when you wear something, hang it the right way. After six months, the backward hangers show what you don't wear.
  • Five-minute reset each week: rehang, refold, return stray items.
  • Seasonal swap twice a year: rotate seasonal clothes and do a quick edit.

What to do with clothes you let go

  • Sell good-condition and branded pieces on resale apps.
  • Donate to charities and shelters (check what they accept).
  • Swap with friends.
  • Recycle worn-out textiles through take-back schemes; many retailers accept them.

Avoid putting textiles in general waste where you can.

A closet that works for you

An organised closet isn't about Instagram-perfect shelves. It's about seeing what you own, wearing more of it and buying less. Once you've edited down to pieces you like, take the next step with a capsule wardrobe, and track which items earn their place with cost per wear.
